Google Ads vs Facebook Ads for HVAC Companies:
Which One Books More Jobs?

Google books urgent repairs. Meta fills planned work. Here’s where to start, and what to fix before you spend.

November 20, 2026 · By Chris Karasewich, Founder

For most HVAC companies, Google Search Ads book more jobs, because they reach people who need a furnace or AC fixed right now. Facebook and Instagram ads (Meta Ads) work better for maintenance plans, seasonal tune-ups, retargeting and staying top of mind before the busy season. AdEasy, a Winnipeg-based marketing leadership partner for growing home-service brands across Canada and the U.S., usually starts HVAC clients on Google, then adds Meta once the website and call tracking are working.

Is Google Ads or Facebook advertising better for getting more HVAC customers?

It depends on the job you want to fill. The two platforms reach people at different moments, so the better question is which moment matters most to your schedule this month.

  • If you need more repair and replacement calls, start with Google Search Ads.
  • If you need to fill the shoulder season with tune-ups, sell maintenance plans or stay in front of past website visitors, add Meta Ads.
  • If your website hides the phone number or nobody can tell which calls came from ads, fix that before you spend on either one.

How is Google Ads different from Facebook Ads?

Google Search Ads work on intent. Someone types “furnace repair Winnipeg” or “no heat emergency” and your ad shows up above the results. That person already has a problem and is ready to call someone. You pay when they click.

Meta Ads work on interruption. Your ad shows up while someone scrolls Facebook or Instagram. Most of them don’t need you today. You’re reaching homeowners in your service area based on location and interests, and your offer has to be good enough to make them stop and act.

Neither one is better in general. Google catches demand that already exists. Meta helps create it, or brings back people who visited your site and didn’t call.

What is Google Ads good for in HVAC?

Google is where homeowners go when something breaks. In a Winnipeg cold snap, the furnace that quits at 10 PM turns into a search on a phone within minutes. If your ad and your Google Business Profile are there, with a number they can tap, you have a real shot at that job.

Google Search Ads work well for:

  • No-heat and no-cool repair calls
  • Furnace, boiler and AC replacement quotes
  • Specific services you want more of, like heat pumps or ductless systems
  • Showing up in towns you serve where you don’t rank on Google Maps yet

Google Local Services Ads are worth a look too. They sit at the top of the page with the “Google Guaranteed” badge, and you pay per lead instead of per click. They work best alongside Search Ads, not as a replacement, and you should still dispute leads that aren’t real customers.

What is Facebook advertising good for in HVAC?

Meta Ads are useful when the customer doesn’t have an emergency yet. That makes them a good fit for the work you want to plan around:

  • Fall furnace tune-ups and spring AC check-ups
  • Maintenance plan sign-ups
  • AC pre-season offers before the first hot week
  • Retargeting people who visited your site but didn’t call or book
  • Showing your crew, your trucks and your reviews to homeowners in your area

Meta can also reach past customers, as long as you have their permission to market to them. A seasonal reminder to people who already trust your work usually costs less than finding someone new.

Which should you start with? A simple decision table

Your situation Start with Why
Repair calls are slow and crews have open time Google Search Ads Reaches people who need service today
You want more replacement and install quotes Google Search Ads High-value searches with clear intent
Tune-up and maintenance schedule has gaps Meta Ads Puts a seasonal offer in front of homeowners before they search
You’re launching or growing a maintenance plan Meta Ads, plus email or text to past customers Plans sell on trust and repetition, not urgency
Lots of website visitors, few calls Neither yet Fix the website and tracking first
Strong Google results and a full repair schedule Add Meta Ads Fill slower weeks and keep your name in front of homeowners

How should you split your budget between Google and Facebook?

There’s no single right split, because it depends on your season, your service area and what your crews can take on. A practical starting point for most HVAC companies is to put most of the budget into Google Search Ads, then test Meta with a smaller amount aimed at one clear offer, like a fall tune-up.

Shift the budget based on results, not clicks. If Meta tune-up campaigns are booking real appointments at a cost per booked job you’re happy with, give them more. If they’re bringing in form fills that never turn into work, pull back and fix the offer.

Seasons matter in Canada. Expect to lean harder on Google during cold snaps and heat waves, and use Meta more in the shoulder seasons when you want to fill the schedule on your terms.

What should be in place before you spend on either one?

Ads send people to your website. If the website can’t turn a visitor into a phone call, both platforms will waste money. Before you launch, check that you have:

  1. A separate page for each main service you advertise (furnace repair, AC install, heat pumps)
  2. A phone number that’s one tap away on mobile
  3. Call tracking, so you know which calls came from Google and which from Meta
  4. Someone answering the phone during the hours your ads run
  5. Forms that reach a real person who replies the same day

Many HVAC companies already have most of this. The gaps are usually call tracking and missed calls during busy hours.

How do you know which one is working?

Clicks and likes don’t pay the bills. Judge each platform by qualified leads and booked jobs.

At AdEasy, we count a qualified lead as a real person with a genuine need for a service you offer, in an area you serve, asking about getting that work done. Spam, job applicants, sales calls and duplicate inquiries about the same job don’t count. Every month, our clients get a Marketing Dashboard that shows what their spend produced in qualified leads and, where they share the data, booked jobs.

That’s how a long-term client like Clean Line Plumbing, Heating and Electrical in Winnipeg decides where the budget goes: based on which channels bring in real customers, not which ones look busy.

FAQ

Is Google Ads or Facebook advertising better for getting more HVAC customers?

+

For repair and replacement calls, Google Search Ads usually book more jobs, because they reach people who need service now. Facebook and Instagram ads work better for tune-ups, maintenance plans and retargeting. Most growing HVAC companies end up using both.

Can Facebook Ads work for emergency HVAC calls?

+

Rarely. People with no heat search Google, they don’t scroll Facebook waiting for an ad. Use Meta for planned work and use Google for urgent calls.

Are Google Local Services Ads worth it for HVAC?

+

They can be. You pay per lead, and the Google Guaranteed badge helps with trust. Track them like any other channel and dispute leads that aren’t real customers.

How long does it take to see results?

+

Google Search Ads can bring calls within days if the website and tracking are ready. Meta campaigns usually need a few weeks of testing to find an offer that books appointments.

Should I run ads myself or hire someone?

+

You can run them yourself if you have time to manage bids, offers and tracking every week. If you’d rather run your shop, hand the marketing to someone accountable for the whole plan: the ads, the website they land on, the tracking and the budget. Ask them to report on qualified leads and booked jobs, not just clicks.
