We Listened to 704 Plumbing Calls.
Here’s What Counts as a Real Lead.

Your ad platform counts every call as a lead. About half of them were real customers. Here’s the definition we use and how to sort your own calls.

November 20, 2026 · By Chris Karasewich, Founder

A qualified lead is a real person or business, with a real need, for a service you offer, in an area you serve, asking to get it done. Everything else your ad platform calls a “lead” is noise you’re paying to count. This post walks through a real call review we did for a Winnipeg plumbing company, the definition we use, and how you can sort your own calls in an afternoon.

Why doesn’t your lead count match your booked jobs?

Google Ads, Facebook and most call tracking tools count a lead every time the phone rings or a form comes in. That number goes in the report, and the report looks great.

Then you look at the schedule and it doesn’t add up. That’s because the count includes:

  • Existing customers calling about an invoice or a tech who’s on the way
  • Job applicants and sales calls from other vendors
  • Spam and robocalls
  • Callers outside your service area
  • The same customer calling three times about one job
  • People asking for work you don’t do

None of those are bad calls for your office to take. They just aren’t marketing results, and they shouldn’t be counted as one.

How did we review 704 calls?

In August 2026, a plumbing company we work with in Winnipeg received 704 tracked calls. We didn’t stop at the call count. We reviewed every recorded call, using transcripts and call summaries, and gave each one an outcome:

  1. Booked: the caller scheduled a job.
  2. Callback requested: a real customer who needed a quote or a follow-up call.
  3. Not booked: a real request that didn’t turn into a job (no availability, price, timing).
  4. Not a lead: existing-customer admin, applicants, vendors, spam, wrong numbers and repeat calls about the same job.

We also split new callers from returning callers. A returning caller isn’t always a new lead. Sometimes it’s a customer you already have, calling about the job you’re already doing.

What did we find?

About half of the 704 calls were real service requests from customers the company could actually serve. The other half were real phone calls, but not new business: returning customers checking on a job, admin questions, calls that weren’t for plumbing work, and calls nobody could have booked.

That doesn’t mean the marketing was failing. It means the “lead” number in the ad report was roughly twice as high as the number that mattered. If you judge your marketing on the bigger number, you’ll make budget decisions on bad math.

What counts as a qualified lead for a plumbing company?

A call or form counts as a qualified lead when all five are true:

Check Counts Doesn’t count
A real person or business Homeowner, property manager, business owner Spam, robocalls, vendors selling to you
A real need A leak, a clog, a new install, a quote Job applicants, invoice questions
For a service you offer Drain cleaning, water heaters, sewer lines Work you refer out
In an area you serve Your city and the towns you drive to Callers three hours away
Asking to get it done Booking, requesting a quote or callback The same customer calling twice about one job (counts once)

Write this down, and make sure everyone who reports on your marketing uses the same definition. If your Google Ads report, your call tracking and your office all count leads differently, nobody can tell you what’s working.

How do you run your own call review?

You don’t need special software to start. Most call tracking tools (CallRail, for example) give you recordings and an export.

  1. Export last month’s calls from your call tracking tool.
  2. Remove calls under about 20 seconds and obvious spam.
  3. Listen to or read the summary of each remaining call, and mark it booked, callback requested, not booked or not a lead.
  4. Mark new and returning callers.
  5. Count qualified leads by source: Google Ads, Google Business Profile, website, Facebook, referrals.
  6. Compare that to what each source cost you last month.

The first review takes an afternoon. After that, 30 to 60 minutes a month keeps it current.

What should you do with the answer?

Once you know how many real customers each channel brings in, the decisions get simpler:

  • Cost per qualified lead tells you which channels are worth more budget, not cost per call.
  • A high “not booked” count is usually a booking problem, not a marketing problem. Your team owns that fix: who answers, how fast, and what they say.
  • A high “not a lead” count from one campaign is a targeting problem. That one belongs to whoever runs your ads.

Who should own this number?

Your marketing lead should. Every month, someone needs to sort the calls and forms, report qualified leads by channel, and tie them to booked revenue in a dashboard you can read in five minutes.

When nobody leads the marketing, this job falls to the owner at 9 PM, or it doesn’t get done at all. The ad vendor reports its own numbers, the website company reports traffic, and nobody is accountable for which numbers you trust. That’s the gap a marketing leader fills. FAQ

What is a qualified lead for a plumbing company?

+

A real person or business with a real plumbing need, for a service you offer, in an area you serve, who is asking to book, get a quote or get a callback. Spam, job applicants, existing-customer admin calls and repeat calls about the same job don’t count.

Why does Google Ads report more leads than I booked?

+

Google Ads counts conversions, such as calls over a set length or form submissions. It can’t tell a new customer from an existing one, a job applicant or a caller outside your area. Reviewing the calls shows how many were real.

What percentage of calls are qualified leads?

+

It varies by company and channel. In the 704 calls we reviewed for a Winnipeg plumbing company, about half were real service requests. Your number will depend on your mix of new and returning customers and how your ads are set up.

How often should I review my calls?

+

Monthly. The first review takes an afternoon. After that, 30 to 60 minutes a month is enough to keep your qualified lead numbers accurate.

Should form submissions be counted the same way?

+

Yes. Apply the same five checks to every form, remove duplicates and spam, and count each customer once per job.
