How to Get Your HVAC Company Higher on Google Maps:
A Winnipeg Guide

What actually moves the map rankings for furnace and AC companies in Winnipeg and across Canada, and what to skip.

November 18, 2026 · By Chris Karasewich, Founder

To rank higher on Google Maps, you need a complete, accurate Google Business Profile; a steady flow of new reviews; a website with a page for every service you want to rank for; and the same name, address, and phone number everywhere online. How do I get my HVAC company to show up higher on Google Maps?

Google ranks your map results on three things: how relevant your business is to the search, how close you are to the person searching, and how well known and trusted your business looks online. You can’t move your shop closer to every customer, but you can control your shop’s relevance and trust.

When the temperature drops to -30C in January, and a furnace quits at 10 PM, the homeowner grabs their phone and searches “furnace repair near me” or “furnace emergency.” They call one of the first three companies on the map. Here’s how to earn one of those spots.

Is your Google Business Profile set up for the work you want?

Start with the profile itself. Most HVAC profiles we review are missing at least half of these Google Business Profile features:

  1. Primary category. Pick the one that matches your main money-maker, usually “HVAC contractor” or “Heating contractor.” Add secondary categories like “Air conditioning contractor” or “Furnace repair service” only if you do that work.
  2. Services. List each service separately: furnace repair, furnace installation, AC tune-ups, boiler service, heat pumps, duct cleaning. Write a short, plain description for each.
  3. Service area. Add the cities and neighbourhoods you actually drive to, like Winnipeg, Headingley, East St. Paul and Steinbach. Don’t list places you won’t go.
  4. Hours. Keep them accurate, and add holiday hours. If you offer 24/7 emergency service, say so in your hours and description, and make sure someone answers.
  5. Photos. Upload real photos of your techs, vans, installs and your shop every month. Skip stock photos.
  6. Posts and Q&A. Post a seasonal update once or twice a month (fall furnace tune-ups, spring AC checks). Answer the common questions yourself in the Q&A, like “Do you service older boilers?”

How do reviews help your map ranking?

Reviews are one of the biggest trust signals Google uses, and the date matters as much as the total. A company with 400 reviews and nothing new since spring will often lose to a company with 150 reviews and a few new ones every week.

A simple routine works best:

  • The tech asks in person, after the job is done, before leaving the house. This is when the customer is happiest and most likely to leave a review.
  • The office texts a direct review link the same day after the job is complete.
  • Every tech wears an ID badge with a Google review QR code, so the customer can leave a review on the spot.
  • Someone replies to every review, good or bad, within a few days. Mention the service in your reply when it fits (“Glad we got your furnace running again before the cold snap”).

Never buy reviews or offer discounts for them. Google removes them, and it can suspend your profile.

Does your website affect where you show up on Google Maps?

Yes. Google checks your website to confirm what you do and where you do it. If your site has one “Services” page that lists everything in a paragraph, Google has very little to work with.

Give each main service its own page: furnace repair, furnace replacement, AC installation, heat pumps, boilers. If you serve several towns, add a page for each one with real details about the work you do there, not the same page with the city name swapped. Make sure your phone number is one tap away on mobile, because people who find you on the map will usually check your site before they call.

Is your business information the same everywhere?

Your company name, address and phone number should match exactly on your Google Business Profile, your website, Facebook, Yelp, the Better Business Bureau, HomeStars and any directories you’re listed on. Small differences (“Ave” vs “Avenue” is fine, two different phone numbers are not) make it harder for Google to trust which details are right.

This matters when you use call tracking. To track calls from your profile without hurting your ranking, set the tracking number as the primary phone number on Google Business Profile and add your main business number as an additional phone number. That way, you can see how many real customers the map brings in each month, and Google still connects your profile to the number listed everywhere else.

What doesn’t help your Google Maps ranking?

Some tactics still get sold to HVAC owners. Skip them:

  • Stuffing keywords into your business name, like “Smith Heating Furnace Repair Winnipeg Best HVAC.” Competitors can report it, and Google can suspend the profile.
  • Fake addresses or virtual offices to rank in more cities. Google verifies locations and removes these profiles.
  • Buying reviews or posting reviews from staff and family.
  • Paying for hundreds of low-quality directory listings.

If someone guarantees you the number one spot on Google Maps, keep your wallet in your pocket. Nobody controls the map.

How long does it take to move up on Google Maps?

Fixing the profile and the website pages can show results within a few weeks. Reviews and trust build over months. Plan for 3 to 6 months of steady work before you judge it, and start before furnace season. If you start in October, you’re catching up while your competitors are already on the map when the first cold snap hits.

The work doesn’t stop once you rank. Spring AC tune-ups, fall furnace checks, and the January rush each need fresh posts, photos and reviews. That’s why word of mouth alone can’t keep your schedule full; we covered that in How Home Service Companies Get More Consistent Work.

FAQ

How do I get my HVAC company to show up higher on Google Maps?

+

Complete your Google Business Profile with the right primary category, services, service area and hours, get new reviews every week, build a website page for every service you want to rank for, and keep your name, address and phone number the same everywhere online.

What is the best Google Business Profile category for an HVAC company?

+

Use the category that matches the work you most want, usually “HVAC contractor” or “Heating contractor.” Add secondary categories only for services you actually offer.

How many Google reviews does my HVAC company need?

+

There’s no magic number. A steady flow of new reviews matters more than a big total. Aim for a few new reviews every week, and reply to all of them.

Can I rank on Google Maps in cities where I don’t have an office?

+

You can show up in nearby areas through your service area settings, city pages on your website and reviews from customers there. Don’t create fake addresses to rank in other cities.

Can I use a call tracking number on my Google Business Profile?

+

Yes. Set the tracking number as the primary number and your main business number as an additional number. That keeps your listings consistent and shows you how many calls the map brings in.
