01 · The Situation

Plenty of Giving.
Not Much Showing.

RE/MAX supports a long list of local causes: Children’s Miracle Network, the Teddy Bears’ Picnic, rinkside boards at Winnipeg Ice games, the Goldeyes, Assiniboia Downs and a float in the Santa Claus Parade. The money comes from the agents themselves, who pay into the fund every month.

The agents want to see where their money goes, and homeowners should know RE/MAX shows up for the community. There’s also a rule: this work can’t promote any one agent. It has to be about the community.

In 2021 we made RE/MAX a community support video that rounded up a year of that work. Then RE/MAX decided to sponsor a Dream Bike.

What RE/MAX needed
Show agents where their money goes
Reach homeowners in three communities
Keep it about the community, not one agent
02 · The Dream Bike Program

Every Kid Should
Get to Ride.

A child living with a physical disability often can’t ride a regular bike. An adaptive bike is built around the child who will ride it. In 2020, AdEasy started the Dream Bike Program with Freedom Concepts, a Winnipeg company that has built adaptive bikes since 1991, to find local businesses willing to pay for one.

Clean Line sponsored the first bike in October 2020, and our own team raised the money for another. In 2021, RE/MAX signed on to sponsor Milo’s.

The cost

$4,800+ Per Bike

Each adaptive bike is custom built for the child who will ride it, so no two are alike and none of them come cheap.

The gap

Often Not Covered

Many government programs don’t pay for this kind of equipment, which leaves families to find the money on their own.

The fix

A Business Sponsors It

The sponsor pays for a child’s bike, gets a charitable receipt through a local charity partner, and a photo of the new rider.

04 · The Results

Seen by 135,000
Neighbours.

The Facebook campaign launched in December 2021. By late February 2022 it had reached 135,899 people in Winnipeg, Stonewall and Steinbach and had been shown 821,231 times, on $2,225 in media spend.

That’s less than two cents for each person reached. Meta estimates 14,200 of them would still remember the ad two days later. For less than half of what the bike itself cost, the story of where the agents’ money went reached more than 135,000 neighbours.

The same goes for a home-service company that sponsors a team, a hamper drive or a bike. The giving matters. So does making sure the people you serve see it.
