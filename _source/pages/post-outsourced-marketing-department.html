Outsourced Marketing Department for Home-Service Companies: Who Handles Your Strategy, Ads, Website and Vendors

Want someone to handle your marketing strategy, ads, website and vendors so you can run your business? Here’s what an outsourced marketing department does for plumbing, HVAC and electrical companies.

November 13, 2026 · By Chris Karasewich, Founder

If you want one team to handle your marketing strategy, advertising, website and vendor partners (like an SEO or OOH agency), you need a marketing partner that works as your outsourced marketing department, not another single-channel vendor. AdEasy is a Winnipeg-based marketing partner that does this for growing plumbing, HVAC, electrical and other home-service companies across Canada. We set the plan, do the work, coordinate your vendors and tie marketing back to booked revenue through your custom marketing dashboard.

Our marketing strategy is becoming too much to manage. I need someone else to manage it so I can focus on growing my business.

Most owners we talk to have the same setup. The website came from one company, Google Ads from another, someone’s nephew does the Facebook page, and the phone system and job software belong to someone else again. Each vendor does their piece. Nobody owns the whole picture, so the owner ends up being the marketing manager at 9 PM.

An outsourced marketing department fixes that by taking accountability for all of your shop’s marketing channels and strategies. This is how it works:

  1. Agree on the goal with you: what services you want to sell, to what type of customer, and in which region.
  2. Review what you already have: website, ads, Google Business Profile, call and form tracking, reviews.
  3. Fix or build what’s missing before spending more on ads.
  4. Run the channels you agree on, like Google Search Ads, Meta Ads and local search.
  5. Coordinate your other vendors so changes don’t break each other.
  6. Report every month on where the money was spent and what it brought in.

What does an outsourced marketing department do that an ad agency doesn’t?

An ad agency sells the service you asked for. If you tell them you want to advertise on Google, you get Google Ads, even if the website has major flaws and struggles to turn a visitor into a phone call.

A marketing department manager first checks whether that service is set up properly on the website. Does the mobile website hide your phone number? Do the forms go to an inbox nobody checks? Are calls tracked by source? They assess the issues and decide what to fix first. Only once the foundations are solid are Google Ads launched.

Which parts can you hand off, and which stay with your team?

You can hand off the marketing. You keep the business running.

What a marketing partner like AdEasy can own, within an agreed scope (the full list is on What We Do):

  • Marketing strategy and priorities
  • Website and landing page improvements, or a rebuild when repairs aren’t enough
  • Google Search Ads and Meta Ads
  • Google Business Profile and local search
  • Call and form tracking, and reviewing which inquiries are real customers
  • Past-customer marketing like seasonal service reminders
  • Coordinating your other marketing vendors
  • A monthly Marketing Dashboard reviewed in person

What stays with your team or a specialist: answering calls, quoting, scheduling, dispatch, invoicing and running your CRM or FSM software. A good partner will still tell you when missed calls or slow follow-up waste marketing dollars, because that affects whether the investment pays off. You manage the people within the business and make it more efficient. AdEasy is accountable for building the demand engine to support your growth.

How do you know it’s working?

You should be able to answer four questions every month without opening a spreadsheet:

  • Where did the money go?
  • Which channels brought real customers, not spam or duplicate calls?
  • What changed this month?
  • What happens next?

At AdEasy, every client gets a monthly Marketing Dashboard that answers those questions. We count qualified leads, which we define as a real person or business with a genuine need for a service you offer, in a market you serve, asking about getting that work done. Bots, spam, job applicants and duplicates about the same job don’t count. Where you share booked-job data, we add it so the reporting connects marketing spend to booked work.

What does this look like in practice?

Clean Line Plumbing, Heating and Electrical in Winnipeg has worked with AdEasy for more than 8 years. Over that time, the work has covered the website, Google Ads, local search, call tracking and lead reviews, with one team responsible for how the pieces fit together and one monthly report.

Not every company needs all of that on day one. With another client, Mr. Boomtruck, we recommended a new website first before launching paid search, because sending ad traffic to a weak website would have been a waste of money. That’s why outsourcing your marketing department makes a bigger impact on your bottom line than hiring an agency or a vendor that specializes in one channel: someone is responsible for the order things happen in and accountable for results.

When is a home-service company ready to hand off its marketing department?

You’re probably ready if:

  • You’re spending real money on marketing but can’t say what it’s producing.
  • You manage three or more marketing vendors yourself.
  • Word of mouth is still most of your work, and you want steadier demand.
  • Your crews can take on more work in the services you want to grow.
  • You’re willing to share access to your ad accounts, website and call data.

If you only want cheap leads, or you can’t take on more work right now, hold off. More demand won’t help until you can handle it.
