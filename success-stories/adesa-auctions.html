Success Stories · ADESA Auctions (now OPENLANE) · 2018 to 2024

Ten Cities.
One Lead Engine.

ADESA Auctions Canada runs wholesale vehicle auctions from Vancouver to St. John’s. Dealers buy there, fleets sell there, and everyday car owners can sell there too, if they know it’s an option.

From 2018 to 2024, AdEasy managed ADESA’s marketing across ten Canadian cities, right through a national rebrand to OPENLANE.

01 · The Situation

Winnipeg Isn’t Vancouver.
Halifax Isn’t Calgary.

ADESA needed one partner to handle marketing for every location: ads, print, video, events, hiring, community sponsorships and landing pages. The biggest job was the consumer program, which buys cars from everyday owners and sells them to dealers through the auction lanes.

Someone with a car to sell has plenty of options. They can sell it privately, trade it in at a dealer, or use one of the newer apps that come to their door. The auction has to earn the call.

And every market behaves differently. What costs $20 a lead in one city can cost $80 in another. A national campaign would have spent the budget where clicks were cheap, not where the auction lanes needed cars.

Each city also had its own rep working the leads. A lead that sat in a shared inbox was a car sold somewhere else.

02 · What We Did

We Ran Every City,
Like Our Own Business.

01

A Campaign for Every City

Google Search, Meta and Microsoft Ads built city by city, each with its own budget. When one market got expensive, we could fix it without touching the others.

02

Videos That Teach the Process

Our best ads by far were short videos that explained how selling a car at auction works, step by step. Montreal got its own versions in French.

03

Every Lead to the Right Rep

Every lead went straight into a sales pipeline in ActiveCampaign, assigned to the rep in that city, with follow-up emails in English and French. ADESA could see leads by city and by rep.

03 · The Call Audit

We Listened
to the Calls.

The ads promised that selling a car at ADESA was easy, safe and fast. In 2022 we wanted to know if the phone calls said the same thing, so we listened to recorded calls from the reps in each city and reported back to ADESA’s leadership.

The best calls explained the process first and built trust before asking about the car. Others jumped straight to the vehicle details. After the call, sellers still had a form to fill out and a car to get to the auction. Fees were explained one way in one city and another way somewhere else. Transport came up only when a seller asked.

None of that shows up in an ad report. All of it decides whether a lead turns into a car in the lane.

The lesson

An ad can only promise what the phone call delivers.

What we recommended
Get the consignment form done before the call, not after it
Offer vehicle transport up front, not only when a seller asks
Explain fees the same way in every city
Review calls and coach reps on a regular schedule
04 · The Results

957 Sellers
in One Month.

From May 2023 to January 2024, the program brought in more than 6,400 seller leads across ten cities. July 2023 was the biggest month, with 957 leads.

In the third quarter of 2023, the campaigns produced 2,488 leads on $71,660 in ad spend, about $29 each. Google Search carried three in four of those leads. Meta kept sellers seeing the videos, and Microsoft Ads added a few more at low cost.

Every one of those leads landed with a named rep in a named city, so ADESA could see what each market produced.

05 · The Rebrand

A Rocky Rebrand.
Then Back on Track.

In mid-December 2023, ADESA Canada became OPENLANE. We rebuilt the program for the new brand: new ads in every city, new tracking, new follow-up emails, and the email settings that keep those emails out of spam folders.

A new name still means starting over with people who have never heard it. Leads fell about 30% in December and 50% in January compared with the summer and fall average. We told OPENLANE exactly that, and kept working the campaigns month by month.

By June 2024, the seven markets we were still running brought in 753 leads in a month, more than three times January. Cost per lead dropped from about $82 to about $34.
