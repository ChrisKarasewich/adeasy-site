to Marketing Leadership.

2018 · One job: fill the plumbing schedule

A West St. Paul Plumbing Shop
That Needed More Calls.

In 2018 Clean Line was a West St. Paul plumbing shop with a fraction of the team it has today. The ask was simple: more residential plumbing calls, at a cost that made sense. We built them a new website, launched their first Google Ads campaigns and tracked every call and form from day one, so Jeromy could see exactly what each dollar brought in.

Proving It Out.

Clean Line didn’t hand us a big budget, and that’s part of why it worked. Every year, their strategy was led by data. As the campaigns proved themselves, Clean Line put more behind them: ad spend grew from $11,668 in 2018 to $34,328 in 2020, and leads grew with it.

2021 to 2023 · A bigger budget, a bigger stage

Growing
the Brand.

As Clean Line grew, so did its marketing budget, and not all of it belonged in online ads. The money started going to places where people could meet Clean Line in person: events, trade shows, the Winnipeg Jets sponsorship, and giving back to the community through programs like the Dream Bike Sponsorship Program.

Spending more is easy. Keeping the brand consistent across every new marketing initiative is the hard part. We planned those investments and produced the photography, advertising and event materials behind them, so a homeowner who saw Clean Line at a Jets game, a trade show booth or a community event saw the same brand they’d find on Google and Meta.

Google Ads kept doing their job underneath it all.

2,442 leads from Google Ads in 2021,
double what year one produced
2024 to 2026 · Same budget, far more calls

More From
Every Dollar.

Growth gets expensive if you just keep raising the budget. So we tightened everything. Campaigns split by intent: residential, commercial, branded and competitors. CallRail on every channel. Monthly reports that tie spend to calls, requests and booked jobs.

The result: ad spend held steady at about $38,000 a year, while leads jumped from 2,518 in 2024 to 3,616 in 2025. That’s 44% more leads for slightly less money. Cost per lead dropped from $15.42 to $10.52, and so far in 2026 it’s $9.21.

We also watch what happens after the click. When call data showed customers ringing through and hanging up, we traced it to a phone setting and got it fixed: missed calls from that problem dropped from about 95 a month to about 10. When Clean Line’s website was hacked in March 2026, we checked every tracking tag after the restore and had the account back to full strength within weeks.

2026 · From leads to qualified leads to booked jobs

Following Calls
to Booked Work.

A lead is anyone who calls or fills out a form. Most marketing reports stop there, because most businesses don’t track what happens next. But a lead isn’t a customer. Some calls are spam, vendors or job seekers. Some are existing customers checking on a tech’s ETA.

So in August 2026 we went through every tracked call Clean Line received, all 704 of them, and sorted each one. Was it a real customer looking to buy? New or returning? Booked on the call, promised a callback, or turned away because there was no truck free that day? Then we paired those rates with Clean Line’s average job value from their FSM and extrapolated the data over the last 12 months.

Where work was slipping away

The audit also showed where work was slipping away after the phone rang. In August alone, 142 qualified calls ended without a booking: callers waiting on a callback, same-day requests with no truck free, and price questions. That was about $70,000 in work for one month, and it gave Clean Line a clear list of what to fix.
